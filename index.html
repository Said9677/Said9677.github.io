MIRAS: a marketplace for Hadramout

Shown as a STAR case study: situation, task, action, result. MIRAS is a real product with a live website. The app source is in a private repository.

SituationThe context

MIRAS is built for how people in Hadramout buy and sell: Arabic first, cash on delivery, and prices in Yemeni rial or Saudi riyal. It already ran as a website on Supabase, and the next step was a mobile app.

TaskWhat I had to deliver

Launch an Android app for customers, merchants and drivers on the same backend as the live website. No duplicated business logic, no new database, and no risk to the production site.

ActionWhat I did

  • Set the rules first. Anything touching money, security, the server or production needs a written plan and my approval before it is built.
  • Built the app in Flutter with customer, merchant and driver roles, Arabic and English strings, and dark mode.
  • Protected cash on delivery with a per-order handover code, and kept customer phone numbers and addresses hidden from merchants.
  • Kept dev and production apart: separate Supabase projects, and the app refuses to start if its build flavor and project do not match.
  • Tested it: 566 automated tests, including guards that block hard-coded text, off-palette colors and unsafe automatic retries on writes. I also tested on a real phone for every change.
  • Prepared the release: signing-key backups, database backups, a privacy policy, a data-safety declaration and an account-deletion path.
  • Coordinated the website: changes go through pull requests, a release manifest with file hashes, and a verifier that runs before production. I used this flow to bring the owner panel's add-product form in line with the merchant form.

ResultWhere it stands

  • Version 1.0.0 is published to the Google Play internal testing track and is available to invited testers.
  • The closed-testing phase (12 testers for 14 days) is the next gate, and I am recruiting testers now.
  • The owner panel now adds products with the same currency, condition, type and location options merchants have. It is live on the production site.
  • Project rules keep secrets out of the repositories, and every release gate is written down.

The app is not yet in public release, so there are no public download or user numbers to quote.
